Pokémon TCG - Beginner Rules

Pokémon is a trading card game where players use Pokémon, Energy and Trainer cards to battle. Your goal is to Knock Out your opponent's Pokémon and collect all your Prize cards.

1. Your Objective

You win by meeting one of these conditions:

  • Collect all 6 of your Prize cards.
  • Your opponent has no Pokémon remaining in play.
  • Your opponent cannot draw a card at the beginning of their turn because their deck is empty.

2. Your Deck

A standard Pokémon deck contains exactly 60 cards.

  • You can normally have up to 4 cards with the same name.
  • Basic Energy cards are an exception and can be included in any quantity.
  • Your deck can contain Pokémon, Energy and Trainer cards.

Card Types

Pokémon

Pokémon are your main battling cards. They have attacks, HP, Weakness, Resistance and sometimes Abilities.

Energy

Energy cards are attached to your Pokémon to pay for the Energy costs of their attacks and abilities.

Trainer Cards

Trainer cards help you during the game. They have different types:

  • Item: Cards that provide an effect when played. You can normally play as many Items as you want during your turn.
  • Supporter: Powerful cards that provide useful effects. You can normally play only 1 Supporter per turn.
  • Stadium: Cards that create effects that apply to both players. You can normally play only 1 Stadium per turn.
  • Pokémon Tool: A card attached to a Pokémon to provide an additional effect.

3. Setting Up the Game

  1. Shuffle your 60-card deck.
  2. Decide who goes first using a coin flip or another agreed method.
  3. Draw 7 cards.
  4. Place a Basic Pokémon face-down in your Active Spot.
  5. You may place up to 5 additional Basic Pokémon face-down on your Bench.
  6. Place the top 6 cards of your deck face-down as your Prize cards.
  7. Reveal your Pokémon and begin the game.

Important: If you do not have a Basic Pokémon in your opening hand, you must reveal your hand, shuffle it back into your deck and draw 7 new cards. This is called a mulligan.

Your opponent may draw an extra card for each mulligan you take.

4. Understanding the Play Area

Active Spot

Your Active Pokémon is your main battling Pokémon. It is the Pokémon that normally attacks and receives attacks.

Bench

Your Bench is where your other Pokémon wait. You can have up to 5 Pokémon on your Bench.

Prize Cards

You begin with 6 face-down Prize cards. When you Knock Out an opponent's Pokémon, you take Prize cards depending on the card and its effects.

Discard Pile

Cards that are discarded or Knocked Out are usually placed here.

5. Your Turn

Every turn has three main parts.

1. Draw a Card

Draw 1 card from the top of your deck at the beginning of your turn.

2. Main Actions

You can perform these actions in any order:

  • Play Basic Pokémon from your hand onto your Bench.
  • Evolve your Pokémon if they meet the evolution requirements.
  • Attach 1 Energy card from your hand to one of your Pokémon.
  • Play Trainer cards.
  • Use Pokémon Abilities.
  • Retreat your Active Pokémon, if you can pay its Retreat Cost.

3. Attack

Choose an attack your Active Pokémon can use and pay its Energy cost. Resolve the attack's damage and effects. After attacking, your turn ends.

6. Energy Cards

Energy cards are required to use most Pokémon attacks. Each attack displays its Energy cost next to its name.

Attaching Energy

You can normally attach 1 Energy card from your hand to one of your Pokémon per turn.

Energy Types

There are different types of Basic Energy, including:

  • Grass
  • Fire
  • Water
  • Lightning
  • Psychic
  • Fighting
  • Darkness
  • Metal

Some attacks also require Colourless Energy. Colourless costs can be paid using any type of Energy.

Energy Example

If an attack costs 2 Water Energy, you need 2 Water Energy attached to that Pokémon to use it.

7. Evolution

Pokémon can evolve into stronger forms. Evolution usually follows this order:

  • Basic: The starting form of a Pokémon.
  • Stage 1: Evolves from a Basic Pokémon.
  • Stage 2: Evolves from a Stage 1 Pokémon.

Evolution Rules

  • You can evolve Pokémon in your Active Spot or on your Bench.
  • You cannot evolve a Pokémon on the same turn it was played.
  • You cannot evolve a Pokémon on the same turn it was already evolved.
  • Neither player can evolve Pokémon during their first turn.
  • Some card effects can allow exceptions to these rules.

When a Pokémon evolves, it keeps its attached Energy cards and other attached cards unless an effect says otherwise.

8. Attacking

Your Active Pokémon can normally use one attack per turn. You must have the required Energy attached to use the attack.

Attack Damage

Each attack displays how much damage it deals. Damage is placed on the defending Pokémon as damage counters.

Knock Outs

If a Pokémon has damage equal to or greater than its HP, it is Knocked Out.

When a Pokémon is Knocked Out, it and its attached cards are placed in the discard pile. The opponent takes the appropriate number of Prize cards.

9. Weakness and Resistance

Weakness

Some Pokémon are weak to certain Energy types. If a Pokémon is hit by an attack from a type it is weak to, the attack's damage is increased according to its Weakness.

Resistance

Some Pokémon resist certain Energy types. If a Pokémon is hit by an attack from a type it resists, the attack's damage is reduced according to its Resistance.

10. Retreating

You can retreat your Active Pokémon and replace it with a Pokémon from your Bench.

  • Pay the Retreat Cost shown on your Active Pokémon.
  • Discard the required Energy cards from that Pokémon.
  • Move your Active Pokémon to the Bench.
  • Choose a Pokémon from your Bench to become Active.

You can normally retreat only once per turn. Some card effects can change or remove Retreat Costs.

11. Special Conditions

Some attacks and effects cause Special Conditions. These affect the Pokémon and can make it harder to battle.

Asleep

An Asleep Pokémon cannot attack or retreat. It may recover during Pokémon Checkup.

Burned

A Burned Pokémon takes damage during Pokémon Checkup. A coin flip is used to determine whether it recovers.

Confused

When a Confused Pokémon attacks, its player flips a coin. If tails, the attack does not happen and the Pokémon receives damage.

Paralyzed

A Paralyzed Pokémon cannot attack or retreat during its next turn. It recovers during Pokémon Checkup after that turn.

Poisoned

A Poisoned Pokémon takes damage during Pokémon Checkup.

Special Conditions normally affect only the Active Pokémon. Moving a Pokémon to the Bench or evolving it removes its Special Conditions.

12. Pokémon Checkup

Pokémon Checkup happens between turns, after one player's turn ends and before the next player's turn begins.

During Checkup, resolve effects such as Poison and Burn, and check whether Special Conditions are removed.

13. Important First-Turn Rules

  • The player going first cannot attack on their first turn.
  • The player going first cannot play a Supporter on their first turn.
  • Neither player can evolve Pokémon during their first turn.
  • Both players can still play Items, attach Energy and use Abilities when allowed.

14. Helpful Tips for Beginners

  • Make sure you have a Basic Pokémon ready for your Active Spot.
  • Attach Energy regularly so your Pokémon can use their attacks.
  • Build up your Bench so you have other Pokémon ready to battle.
  • Pay attention to your opponent's Weakness and Resistance.
  • Remember that you normally only get one Energy attachment and one retreat per turn.
  • Read Trainer cards and Pokémon Abilities carefully. They can provide powerful effects.

Remember: Pokémon card effects can override normal game rules. Always read the card carefully.

15. House Rules

These are our own deck-building rules. They are used for our games and take priority over standard deck-building restrictions where they differ.

Deck Building Ratio

Recommended ratio: 20 / 20 / 20

  • 20 Pokémon
  • 20 Trainer / Supporter / Stadium cards
  • 20 Energy

Note: The 20 / 20 / 20 ratio is not strict. You can adjust the numbers to suit your deck. For example, a 20 / 15 / 25 ratio is also acceptable.

Deck Restrictions

  • 1 Pokémon V
  • 1 Pokémon ex
  • 1 Pokémon-GX
  • 1 Pokémon with no Energy requirement for its attack
  • 4 Type 1 Pokémon
  • 1 Leader card
  • No ridiculously overpowered cards, such as a Charizard with 99,999 HP.

Only Exceptions

  • If you do not own a Pokémon V, you may replace it with another Pokémon ex.

Energy Rules

Basic Energy is replaced with regular Energy cards matching the relevant Pokémon type.

Custom Pokémon

Custom Pokémon cards are allowed, provided they are reasonably balanced and do not give players an unfair advantage.

For example, the custom Chainsaw Man Stone-type Basic Pokémon is allowed.
