UniVersus - Beginner Rules

UniVersus is a card game where you use a Character and a deck of attacks, foundations and other cards to defeat your opponent. You play cards by making checks and try to reduce your opponent's Health to 0.

1. Your Objective

You win by meeting either of these conditions:

  • Reduce your opponent's Health to 0.
  • Your opponent runs out of cards in their deck and cannot complete a required deck cycle.
  • A card effect gives you a special way to win.

2. Your Deck

A standard constructed UniVersus deck contains:

  • 1 Character card: Your chosen Character. It is not part of your 60-card deck.
  • At least 60 cards: Your main deck.
  • Up to 4 copies: You can normally include up to 4 copies of a card with the same name.

Your Character determines your starting Hand Size, Health and available resource symbols.

Resource Symbols

Each card has one or more resource symbols. When building your deck, you choose a symbol your Character has. Your cards must share that symbol, unless a card has a special deck-building rule.

3. Card Types

Character

Your Character represents who you are playing as. It provides your starting statistics and usually has abilities you can use during the game.

Attack

Attacks are used to deal damage to your opponent. They have statistics such as Speed, Damage, Difficulty and a Block Zone.

Foundation

Foundations are generally used to support your attacks and improve your ability to play cards. Many have abilities that can be used by committing them.

Asset

Assets are cards that provide ongoing effects or useful abilities while they are in play.

Action

Actions are cards with abilities that can provide different effects. Follow the timing and costs written on the card.

Backup

Backups are cards that can support your Character and provide additional abilities. Some attacks can target an opponent's Backups instead of their Character.

4. Setting Up the Game

  1. Place your Character card in the Character area.
  2. Shuffle your deck and place it in your deck area.
  3. Determine who goes first.
  4. Draw cards up to your Character's Hand Size.
  5. Each player may mulligan according to the setup rules.
  6. Begin the first player's turn.

Your Character's Health is your starting Health. Your Character's Hand Size determines how many cards you normally draw up to at the start of your turn.

5. Your Turn

Your turn is divided into three main phases.

1. Start Phase

The Start Phase has three steps:

  • Ready Step: Ready your committed cards.
  • Review Step: Resolve effects that happen during the Review Step.
  • Draw Step: Draw cards until you reach your Character's Hand Size.

2. Combat Phase

This is where you play attacks and other cards to pressure your opponent.

  • Play attacks to deal damage.
  • Play foundations and other cards to build your resources.
  • Use card abilities when their timing allows.
  • Your opponent can attempt to block your attacks.

3. End Phase

Resolve effects that happen at the end of your turn. Cards in your Card Pool are moved to the appropriate areas according to the rules and their effects.

6. Understanding Checks

A check is how you determine whether you successfully play a card or perform certain actions.

  1. Announce the card you want to play.
  2. Determine the difficulty you need to meet.
  3. Reveal the top card of your deck.
  4. Use its Check value, along with any modifiers, to see whether you pass.
  5. If you pass, the card is successfully played. If you fail, the attempted card is discarded.

Check Example

You want to play a card with a difficulty of 4. You reveal a card with a Check value of 5.

Because 5 is equal to or higher than 4, you pass the check and successfully play the card.

7. Progressive Difficulty

Every card you successfully play during your turn makes subsequent cards harder to play. This is called Progressive Difficulty.

The more cards you play, the higher the difficulty becomes. This means you need to think carefully about the order in which you play your cards.

8. The Card Pool

Successfully played cards are placed into your Card Pool. This is a temporary area where cards stay during the turn.

At the end of your turn, cards are moved out of the Card Pool according to the game rules:

  • Most cards are placed into your discard pile.
  • Attacks that dealt damage can generally be added to your Momentum instead of being discarded.
  • Some card effects may change where cards go.

9. Attacking

Attacks are one of the main ways to reduce your opponent's Health. Each attack has several important statistics.

Speed

Speed determines how difficult the attack is to block. It is used when your opponent attempts to play a block.

Damage

Damage is how much Health your opponent loses if the attack successfully deals damage.

Difficulty

Difficulty is the value you need to meet when checking to play the attack.

Block Zone

The Block Zone indicates which attack zones can be blocked by that card. Check the zones when choosing a block.

10. Blocking

When your opponent plays an attack, you can attempt to block it using a card from your hand with a suitable Block Zone.

  1. Choose a card from your hand that can block the attack.
  2. Check the difficulty of the block using the attack's Speed, your card's Block Modifier and Progressive Difficulty.
  3. Make a check.
  4. If you pass, the block is successful and the attack is stopped.
  5. If you fail, the block does not stop the attack.

11. Important Ability Keywords

Enhance

An Enhance ability can normally be used during the Enhance Step of an attack. It often improves the attack or provides another effect.

Response

A Response ability can be used when its stated triggering condition happens. The card will explain when it can be used.

Form

A Form ability is an action that can be used during its permitted timing. Follow the timing restriction written on the card.

12. Momentum

Momentum is a resource used to pay for certain card abilities and effects.

Attacks that successfully deal damage can generally be placed into your Momentum instead of your discard pile at the end of the turn.

Some powerful abilities require you to spend Momentum. Make sure you have enough before attempting to use them.

13. Important Tips for Beginners

  • Pay attention to Progressive Difficulty. Playing too many cards can make later checks difficult.
  • Build your foundations early so you have more options later.
  • Think about whether an attack is worth blocking or taking damage from.
  • Keep track of your Momentum, especially if your Character relies on it.
  • Read the timing on abilities carefully. Enhance, Response and Form abilities do not all work at the same time.
  • Remember that card text can change how the normal rules work.

Remember: UniVersus has lots of card-specific interactions. If you are unsure when an ability can be used, check its timing and wording.
